# crates/app: what it does

Each command, its keys and what it shows; entry points are in `STATE.md`.

Home (where it starts; the mark in the header goes back, as does the project's menu, the project's name there, which also shows its folder, opens another and lists the recent ones): New project (Ctrl+N; a name, its folder in `~/Documents/Cuprita`, a blank board of 2 to 16 copper layers (agents: to 32) at the size given or one of KiCad's templates, pictured; writes the KiCad files, then shows the schematic), Open… (Ctrl+O, the system's dialog), Recent (a picture of each board, its folder, when it was edited, where it stood when last open: 23 DRC problems, 3 to route, Ready to make; right-click shows its folder or forgets it; a tip of the day below Open), what it found on this computer (KiCad's libraries, ngspice, Ollama; what to do for one missing), KiCad's demo projects; an empty sheet or board says what to do first.
First steps (a new project opens with it; Ctrl+K › First steps shows or hides it): the nine steps from an empty schematic to the fab's files (add the parts, wire their pins, a footprint for each, bring them onto the board, the outline, place them inside it, route, pass the design rules, export), each ticked off from the project as it stands; the next one says how, with a button for its command and its key; it folds to the next step and closes with its ×. On an empty project (or one with only its parts) it offers the walk-through, also Ctrl+K › First board: twelve steps that light an LED from a coin cell, naming what to add (`battery cell`, `R`, `LED`), how to wire it (saying when the LED is the wrong way round), the resistor's value and the footprints to choose, then the way on from F8 to the fab files; Leave the walk-through ends it.
Opens a board (argument or drag-and-drop; on the desktop a dropped `.kicad_pro` opens the board next to it), fits it (the parts beside it too, where F8 leaves new ones), pans/zooms (Home, KiCad's key, or the button in the tool dock fits the board, sheet or 3D view again; until the hand pans or zooms, the board or sheet stays fitted as its canvas changes size: a window resized, a dock opened, a phone turned), shows load timings.
Workbenches (tabs in the header, Ctrl+1–4; on Wayland the header is the window's title bar: drag it, double click it to maximize, the window's buttons at its right, the edges resize): Schematic, Board, 3D, Make; each canvas has its tools in a dock at its bottom (Select first: lit while no tool is on, a click stops the one that is, as Esc does) and the tool on, with what to do, at its top; the status bar says where the pointer is, in mm or mils (Ctrl+U, KiCad's key, or a click on the units switches every length shown; kept for next time; files keep KiCad's mm), the grid, what the pointer snaps to, the active copper layer and what is picked, then what is left to route, the DRC (in the schematic, ERC) count and whether the edits are saved; the pill in the header counts what Make still needs (2 to fix, or Ready to make; the schematic beside the board is read for it a second after the board shows), a click shows Make. Make: how many checks pass and what is left, in one sentence, beside Export (Ctrl+P); before you order (ERC, the board matching the schematic, else how many changes F8 would bring with Update, parts on the board (an empty one isn't ready), a closed outline and its size, every connection routed, design rules, zones filled, part numbers for assembly: each saying what it is about, a failing one with Show, Draw, Fill, or Fill in, which shows the first part lacking a number in the schematic); the fab files (what each holds, its size once written; Show opens their folder); the numbers a fab's quote form asks for (size, copper layers, thickness, smallest track and gap, smallest hole, holes, parts on each side) and JLCPCB, PCBWay and OSH Park's order pages (in the browser); the board in 3D as the fab makes it (a click opens 3D); the BOM checklist's first lines.
Docks beside the canvas (one tab each, folding to a rail): Project (the pages by hierarchy with their parts counted, a click shows one; the board, and how far behind its schematic, with Update; the fab files written; the project's files: its documents, README first, and the datasheets its parts link to, a click opens one), Layers (every layer plus vias and holes, its colour, an eye to show or hide it; a click on a copper layer makes it the active one, marked in copper and ACTIVE; an inner layer shows its plane's net, In1.Cu · GND; hidden layers seldom needed fold under "N more" (the Fab, paste, mask and glue layers start hidden); sets All, Copper, Front, Back), Parts (A); Inspect (what is picked in numbers; while routing, the track being routed: its net, where it started, the connections left, its layer, width, clearance and the via a layer change drops, then the net so far; a part's place, side, footprint and fields; a track or via; a zone's layers and filled area, and its settings, set in place: its net (a choice of the board's), name, priority, clearance, minimum width, thermal gap, spoke width and pad connection (solid, thermal reliefs, on through-hole pads only, none), lengths in the units shown or typed with their own (`0.2mm`, `8mil`), each change one undo step, the zone refilled alone, re-checked and saved as KiCad writes it (a value it can't take, or a corner dragged where it can't go, says why in red there, while the zone stays picked); the net's length, width, current, impedance; else the board), Checks (D), Ask (?), and IR drop, Changes and BOM while those are on. Net classes (Inspect with nothing picked, below the board's numbers): the project's classes as KiCad's Board Setup keeps them in the `.kicad_pro`, Default first, each with its nets' count, clearance and track width; a click opens one to type its clearance, track width, via size and drill and its differential pairs' width and gap (and, but for Default, its name and its nets: their names or patterns, `VBUS, /USB/D?, /DDR/*`), Delete class takes it away, New class adds one; with a net lit, Class beside its name puts the net in a class. Two nets keep the larger of their classes' clearances in DRC, routing, shoving and zone fills (zones are filled again with B), a net's new tracks and vias take its class's sizes; written to the project file at once, the last kept as `-bak` (agents: `netclass`). Desktop only.
Click an item (or Find a net) to highlight its net and inspect length, width, IPC-2152 current and impedance (on a part, only the pad under the pointer lights its net); Esc or an empty click clears.
Fields (Inspect, a part selected in the schematic): every field of the part typed in place (Value, Datasheet, MPN, LCSC…; MPN and LCSC, which assembly reads, offered empty to a part with neither): Enter or leaving it sets it on every unit and sheet (one undo step), Esc keeps it; Footprint: Choose… lists KiCad's footprints that fit the symbol's own filters (`R_*`), narrowed by words typed (`0603`), the current library first, and draws the pads of the one under the pointer; a click sets it, so F8 can add the part. MCP `field` reads and sets them.
Find or run (Ctrl+K or Ctrl+F, the field in the header): every command that can run now, with its key, every part and net of the view shown, and the projects to open (the recent ones; on Home, KiCad's examples too), by a few letters of its name (`rou` → Route, `da3` → net DA3, `u24` → U24, `pic` → pic_programmer); ↑↓ choose, Enter runs the command, zooms to the part (selected) or net (lit) or opens the project, Esc closes.
Place: a click selects a part (cyan box): on a pad, or in its box where no via or track shows on top; drag it to move it (pad outlines show where it lands: its anchor, or the pad grabbed, on the grid; Esc cancels), R / Shift+R turn it 90°, F (KiCad's key, or its button in Inspect) puts it on the other side, mirrored as KiCad's Flip does (`crates/core/save-flip.md`; the status bar says `bottom`); the arrow keys nudge it a grid step; while it is dragged, a dashed guide runs to the nearest part it lines up with each way, with the spacing. Each edit redraws and re-runs DRC.
Pick several, as in the schematic: Shift+click adds a part, track, via or zone to what is picked, or takes it away; Shift+drag anywhere on the board draws a box: left to right it picks what it holds, right to left what it touches (KiCad's rule), on the copper shown; Inspect and the status bar say how many of each (`14 parts, 195 tracks, 40 vias`), with the turn, flip and delete buttons. A drag from any of them moves them all by one step, the one grabbed landing on the grid (shown as it goes; Esc cancels); R / Shift+R turn them 90° about the grid point in their middle, the arrow keys nudge them, Delete deletes them, F puts them on the other side mirrored together when they are all parts (else it flips the view, and the status says why); each is one undo step. Tracks move as they are (KiCad's M): those not picked stay, and the ratsnest and DRC show what broke. Esc or a click on empty board clears.
Add part (`A`, KiCad's key, or the button): a panel finds parts in KiCad's footprint libraries (an installed KiCad's, else the copy of KiCad 10's symbols, footprints and 3D models Cuprita's installers carry), nothing to set up, by the words typed (`MountingHole M3`, `TestPoint`, `Fiducial`, `OSHW logo`); ↑↓ pick, Enter or a click adds it in the middle of the view with the next free reference (`H3`, `TP2`, `FID1`, `LOGO1`), selected to drag into place and turn with R; Esc closes. A again over the canvas (the board's or the sheet's) readies the field for the next part, its words selected. The project's own libraries (its `sym-lib-table`, `fp-lib-table`) come first. A part's KiCad files from a vendor's site (SnapMagic, Ultra Librarian, SamacSys: a zip, `.kicad_sym`, `.kicad_mod`, `.step`), picked with Add part files… (the Parts tab's button, the palette; the system's dialog opens in the downloads folder) or dropped on the window, go into the project's own library (`libraries/<project>.kicad_sym`, `.pretty`, `.3dshapes`, which KiCad reads too; a part there already stays, another of its name goes in as `_2`), and Parts opens on the part (its `lib:name` typed), Enter to add it; for a part number (one word with a digit), Parts links to those sites' search for it. A 3D model alone (a vendor's STEP), picked with Inspect's 3D › Choose… or added with a part picked on the board, goes into the project's `3dshapes` and becomes that part's model, one undo step, saved into the board as KiCad writes models. Desktop only (`crates/core/library.md`, `parts.md`).
New part… (the Parts tab's button, beside Add part files…, or the palette): a window that makes a part of your own: its name, reference letters, description and datasheet; its pins in a table (number, name, KiCad's electrical type, side; N pins and Fill make them at once); its footprint made from a package (SOIC, TSSOP, DFN, QFN, LQFP, pin headers in 1 or 2 rows, DIP: a preset's numbers, each one set in mm or mils, an exposed pad the last pin) or picked from the libraries as Inspect's Choose… picks one; its symbol and footprint drawn as they are typed, what is wrong said below them (Create waits for it). Create puts both into the project's own library, as an import does, the symbol linked to the footprint, and Parts opens on it, Enter to add it; Esc leaves a field typed in, then closes. Agents: `new_part`. Desktop only (`crates/core/newpart.md`).
Delete: a click on a track (cyan glow), via (cyan ring) or zone (on its fill or outline, where no part, pad, via or track is; its outline drawn with its handles) selects it, to drag, turn and nudge as a part; Delete or Backspace deletes what is picked: parts (with their pads, graphics and texts), tracks, vias, zones (with their fills, the net lit; the rest of the file as it was), or, with the pointer on a corner of the selected zone, that corner.
Tracks: a track picked alone drags with its neighbours following, as KiCad's drag: only the move across it counts, each end slides along its neighbour, which keeps its direction, an end held by a pad, via or branch gets a new 45° stretch to it, a loose end goes along (shown as it goes; dragged so far that a track would turn round, it says so and stays); picked with others it moves with them. U (KiCad's key, or its button in Inspect) picks the whole run of the tracks picked, their segments end to end up to pads, vias and branches; Inspect's Width sets the width of the track, or of the tracks picked (a run), typed in the units shown or with its own (`0.3mm`, `12mil`), one undo step, re-checked.
Undo (Ctrl+Z) / Redo (Ctrl+Y, Ctrl+Shift+Z) or the buttons, which name the step: every added part, move, turn, route click, via, delete, zone and fill; a track being routed stops, the selection goes.
Grid: quiet dots on the board, 0.25 mm apart to start (through KiCad's grid origin, when the board sets one); `N` (KiCad's key) steps it on through 1, 0.5, 0.25, 0.1, 0.05 mm, then 100, 50, 25, 10, 5 mil, Shift+N back; the status bar shows it (a click steps it). Zoomed out, every 2nd, 4th, 10th… dot shows, never closer than 10 points. Parts dragged or added land on it; zone, outline and measure points snap to it, or to the centre of the pad or via, or the track end, under the pointer (ringed and named); the route tool aims at it off its net's copper. Ctrl (KiCad's key) frees them all; Shift still frees a corner's 45°.
Help (F1, or the ? on Home): the guide (`docs/`: install, a first board, the schematic, the board, Make, analysis, Ask and agents, working with KiCad; a page's links open the others), where it opens on Home, and every command with its keys and what it does, by where it works (anywhere, the workbenches, board and schematic, the board, while routing, the schematic), filtered by the words typed (`route`, `ctrl+e`, `zone`), where it opens elsewhere; First board and First steps at its top; Esc, F1 or a click outside closes it. Keys read as their keycaps (`/`, `?`) in Help, the palette and tooltips. Settings (Ctrl+, as KiCad's Preferences, or the gear on Home): the units (millimetres or mils, as Ctrl+U), the sheets' page (Dark or Light), motion (Full: the view glides to what Find, Fit or a check shows, a ring runs out from what was found, a workbench fades in, the mark draws itself as the app starts; Reduced: each change at once; kept for next time), the language (as the system's, else one chosen: English, Português (Brasil); the whole window, the guide and all that core says: checks, the router's reasons, refused edits, files' errors; kept for next time) and, on the desktop, the user's own Digi-Key key (Client ID and Secret, kept in Cuprita's data folder in a file only the user can read; the environment's wins), with where Cuprita keeps its things and Show to open the folder.

Drawing helpers: while a tool draws, a crosshair marks the point a click takes, a ring names the pad, pin, via or track end it joins (a diamond the grid point else; the status bar names it too: `snapped to U3 pin 7 · SDA`), a dashed guide carries the track or zone edge being drawn on past it, the way it runs, and a readout by the pointer (clear of the guide) gives the length and angle (and the route's layer and width; a rectangle's size); with no tool, what a click would pick glows first.
Active layer: the copper layer tracks go on (the top one when a board opens): PgUp the top, PgDn the bottom, + the next down the stack, − the one above (KiCad's keys; round from one end to the other), or a click on its row in Layers; it shows, and the status bar names it. It draws above the other copper at full brightness, the other copper dimmer and inner layers (mostly planes) quieter still, but for a highlighted net; copper zones' fills a little quieter than their tracks and pads, which show on them. Pictures (Home, the agent's render) make the side in view's outer copper active.
Route (`X`): click a pad, via or track to start: on a pad through the board or a via, on the active layer; on a pad of one side or a track, on its layer, which becomes active. The track follows the pointer with the clearances (light tint in a soft band as wide as its clearance; a red ring where it can't reach, saying the room it needs), other nets' tracks and vias making way for it as KiCad's Shove does: the stretch of a track in the way moves aside by what the clearance needs, joined to the rest at 45°, a via with its tracks' ends, and what they push in turn, each shown where it goes (dimmed where it was); pads, holes, the edge, arcs, locked items and copper joined to its net along the way stay, and where only moving them would make room (or more than 64 would move) it walks round them, the status saying why (`crates/core/shove.md`). P switches between that and walking round other nets, for the next tracks too; the status bar and the readout say which. A click fixes it with what made way (one undo step), finishing on its net's pad, via or track end; another active layer (inner ones too) fixes it up to the pointer and goes on on that layer from a through via dropped there (none on a pad or via that joins both), V on the other outer layer; Shift+V (Blind vias, on boards of more than two layers) has those vias span only the layers the track goes between, blind or buried, as Inspect says of the route and of a via picked (`crates/core/route.md`); the via makes way as the track does; where it has no room it says why and stays. W and Shift+W (KiCad's keys) step its width through the project's track widths (KiCad's Pre-defined Sizes; else 0.1 to 2 mm) and its net class's, never below the rules' minimum; the readout and the status bar give it. / (KiCad's key) swaps the bend: where the way is clear, its diagonal comes last or first. Enter or a double click finishes; Esc drops the unfixed part. Each fix redraws and re-checks DRC.
Route pair (`6`, KiCad's key): a click on a pad, via or track end of a differential pair's net (named as KiCad pairs them: `D+` and `D-`, `CLK_P` and `CLK_N`, `TX2P` and `TX2N`) takes it and its other net's beside it (within 5 mm); the two tracks follow the pointer side by side, as wide and as far apart as their net class says (never closer than their clearance), round other nets' copper; they leave the pads square, on the side the pointer is; a click fixes a corner, on the pair's other pads it runs in between them and ends each track on its own; Enter or a double click ends it at the pointer, Backspace takes a corner back, Esc drops it. Arriving crossed, a turn too tight or a rule broken is said and nothing laid. One layer; other nets don't make way (`crates/core/pair.md`). The status bar says each side's length as it goes; Inspect's net shows Pair with and Skew.
Tune: a track picked shows its Net length in Inspect; typed longer, the track takes a meander that adds the rest (turns to the side with room, legs three widths apart, up to 1 mm tall, corners cut at 45°), one undo step; the shorter side of a pair has a Match link that makes it as long as the other. Refused, Inspect says why: long enough, too little to add, the track too short for the turns, no room.
Zone (`Z`; KiCad's Ctrl+Shift+Z redoes here): click a pad, via or track: the zone takes its net and layer (as the route tool does); click its corners, each at a multiple of 45° from the last unless Shift is held (outline follows the pointer); Enter or a double click closes it and fills it alone with KiCad's settings for a new zone (`crates/core/fill-new.md`); Backspace drops the last corner, Esc the zone, again the tool. Zone corners: a selected zone has a handle on each corner (a square) and on the middle of each edge long enough (a dot); drag a corner to move it, onto the grid or the pad, via or track end under the pointer (Ctrl frees it), the outline following, or onto the corner beside it to take it away; drag an edge's middle to add a corner there; Delete with the pointer on a corner takes it away (a cutout left with two corners goes). The outline stays one KiCad takes: an edit that would cross an edge, leave a cutout outside or a zone with two corners says so and changes nothing. Each is one undo step ("Zone (GND) on F.Cu: corner moved"); the zone refills alone (zones of lower rank it now overlaps keep their fills until B), redraws and re-checks (`crates/core/fill-new.md`). Cutout (Shift+C, KiCad's key, or its button in Inspect, a zone selected): click the hole's corners inside the zone as the zone tool's (45° unless Shift, Backspace, Enter or a double click closes, Esc drops); it becomes a hole in the zone's outline, saved as KiCad 10 saves a cutout, the zone refilled alone, one undo step; one crossing the outline or another cutout, or round one, is refused and stays drawn to fix; again Shift+C or Esc leaves the tool.
Outline (`O`): drag a rectangle, or click the outline's corners as the zone tool's (45°, Shift frees, Backspace, Enter or double click closes): the board's new outline on Edge.Cuts in place of the outer one, cutouts kept, saved as lines (`crates/core/save.md`); when a part draws part of the outline (an edge connector's tongue) it says so and changes nothing.
Text (Shift+T) and Line (`L`): the board's own words and lines, on the silkscreen of the side shown or the layer chosen in the tool's hint (any but copper, Edge.Cuts, Margin and the courtyards). Text: type the words (`\n` breaks a line), Enter puts them on the grid point in the middle of the view, picked, as large as the project's new texts there, mirrored on a back layer. Line: click its corners as the zone tool's (45°, Shift frees, Backspace; Enter or a double click ends it, open) or drag a rectangle; as wide as the project's lines there. A click picks one of them (a text by its box, a line by its stroke; those a file has on copper or the edge too) where no pad, via or track shows on top, before the part whose box it lies in: drag, the arrows, R and Delete as for a part; Inspect sets a text's words, layer (the other side mirrors it), height, width and pen, a drawing's layer and width, each an undo step. Saved where the file has them, new ones as its KiCad writes them (`crates/core/drawing.md`). A part's own text (its reference, its value, on a layer shown) is picked by a click on it too, alone: a drag and the arrows move it, R turns it where it is (one KiCad keeps upright: level or a quarter turn), Inspect sets its height, width and pen, and Delete hides it (its part keeps it); a part picked lists its Hidden texts in Inspect, the file's too (a reference hidden, a value, a field), and a click on one shows it again, picked to drag into place, its layer shown; it stays where it was put as its part moves on, and saves in its part as KiCad writes a text moved there. Its words stay the schematic's (F8), its layer its footprint's.
Tidy texts (`T`, or the palette; the parts picked, else all): each part's silkscreen reference upright (0° or 90°) at KiCad's size, 1 mm high with a 0.15 mm pen (JLCPCB's smallest; a bigger one keeps its own), at the first spot just outside its courtyard (else its pads' box), the nearest and level first, off other parts' courtyards where it can, clear of solder mask openings, other silkscreen and the board's edge, else hidden; its value on its Fab layer upright in the middle of its body, below the part's reference shown there, both shrunk to fit at KiCad's line pitch, else hidden; one undo step, the status saying how many it placed and hid, saved as KiCad writes texts (`crates/core/tidy.md`). On a sheet, T places every symbol's visible fields beside it, as a new symbol's go: on a side without pins, clear of its pins, wires and labels, on the grid (`crates/core/schematic-fields.md`); one undo step, the status saying how many symbols' moved.
Zones fill again by themselves near each edit that changes their fill (a track routed or shoved, a part moved, turned or deleted…), in that edit's undo step; the status says how many ("1 zone refilled"; `crates/core/fill-new.md`). Fill zones (`B`, KiCad's key, or the button): fills every copper zone again around the copper as it is now (`crates/core/fill.md`); the undo step says how many zones changed ("3 zones filled"), and there is none when no fill changed.
Schematic (`Ctrl+E`, KiCad's key for it, or the button; again for the board): the project's schematic, read from the `.kicad_sch` beside the board (a `.kicad_sch` opened or dropped opens as one; its project's board follows), every sheet as KiCad draws it, in its frame and title block, as the PDF prints it (`crates/core/schematic.md`), on a dark page as the board's canvas (Settings' Sheets: Light shows KiCad's paper; a PDF is always light). Drag pans, the wheel zooms, the left panel shows or hides each kind (wires, labels, pin numbers…); a double click on a sheet opens it, the path above (or Backspace) goes back up; a click on a symbol picks it, its part in Inspect, and Find (Ctrl+F) shows the sheet with a reference (`U3` finds `U3A`) and picks the part's symbols there. Cross-probing: with a part selected, Ctrl+E selects it in the other view (board or schematic) and zooms to it, unless that view already selects it; the status says when that view lacks it (power symbols and parts excluded from the board have nothing there). A click on a wire or label lights its net on every sheet (a glow along its wires, over its labels and power symbols, on its pins; the status names the sheets it reaches, `Net /SDA, on 3 sheets: …`), and Ctrl+E lights it on the board too and zooms to it, as a click on copper lights a net on the board and Ctrl+E lights it on the sheets, showing the page shown or the first it reaches (with a part selected, the part shows and its net lights); by KiCad's net names, which F8 gives the board. ERC (`D`, as DRC on the board, or the button): every electrical rule violation of the project in plain words, as KiCad checks them with the project's severities (`crates/core/erc.md`), errors red, warnings amber; hover one for its fix, a click shows its sheet and marks the place; circles mark those on the sheet shown. Pick, move, turn, delete: a click picks a symbol, a wire (it glows), a label or a no-connect flag (Inspect says what: a part's fields, a wire's length, "3 symbols, 4 wires"); Shift+click adds or takes one away; Shift+drag on empty paper draws a box (left to right it takes what it holds, right to left what it touches, as KiCad); Esc or a click on empty paper clears. A drag from a picked item moves all that is picked, shown as it goes, on the 50 mil grid: what joins it follows (a wire on a pin or end keeps its part up to the first junction or label on it and a new stretch reaches the new place; a T's junction and the labels along a moved wire go with it), so no connection breaks and none is made by the way (a pin, end or label landing on something joins it; where no stretch can be drawn clear, or it would touch a bus or lie along another wire, it says where and stays); a label dragged alone names what it lands on (the status says when that is nothing). R turns what is picked 90° counterclockwise (several about their centre), Delete deletes it (a symbol's part, every unit; junctions left joining fewer than three go too), each one undo step (`crates/core/schematic-move.md`). X mirrors what is picked left to right and Y top to bottom (KiCad's keys), in place as KiCad does: a pin that takes another's place takes its wires (a diode turned round), one that goes to the symbol's other side draws its wires and labels after it. Ctrl+C copies what is picked and Ctrl+V pastes it (Ctrl+X cuts; Ctrl+D duplicates without touching the clipboard): the copy follows the pointer on the grid until a click puts it down, its parts numbered on (`R5` → `R12`, a part's units together), picked to move on; the clip is KiCad's own clipboard text, so what is copied here pastes in KiCad and what KiCad copied pastes here, also into another project (`crates/core/schematic-clip.md`). Page (Inspect with nothing picked): the sheet's paper (A5 to A0, A to E, US sizes; Standing for portrait) and its title block's title, date, revision, company and comments, typed in place, each an undo step. Add symbol (`A` in the schematic, KiCad's key, or the button): the add panel finds symbols in KiCad's installed symbol libraries, nothing to set up, by name, by what they are or by part number (`R`, `LM358`, `regulator 3.3`, `usb c`, `LM358DR`, `STM32F103C8T6`; hover one for its description); a part number also gets a row that asks Digi-Key (desktop; first in the list once Settings, or `DIGIKEY_CLIENT_ID` and `DIGIKEY_CLIENT_SECRET`, hold the user's own free key, else last, saying how to get one) for the exact part: KiCad's symbol for it, else a chip resistor's, ceramic capacitor's or fixed inductor's with its value, and its package's footprint, datasheet, maker and number set (`crates/core/digikey.md`); Enter or a click picks it, and it follows the pointer on the 50 mil grid (R turns it, Esc or Ctrl+Z drops it) until a click puts it down with the next free reference (`R5`; a part's every unit, in a row; `crates/core/schematic-edit.md`). Wire (`W`, KiCad's key, or the button): W picks the tool and a click starts a wire at its grid point (the key alone draws nothing; W again puts the tool away); each click after adds it to there, bent once the longer way first as the preview shows (`/` turns the corner the other way, Shift goes straight, at any angle), and goes on from there; it ends on a pin, wire or label, with a double click or Enter, and Esc ends it (again, the tool); a junction goes wherever KiCad needs one to join a wire's end or a pin to a wire's middle. Bus (`B`, KiCad's key, or the button) draws buses as W draws wires; Bus entry (`Z`, KiCad's key, or the button): a 45° entry follows the pointer on the grid (R turns it) and each click puts one down, joining a wire to a bus; a label on a bus names its members as KiCad reads them (`D[0..7]`, `{SDA SCL}`, `USB{DP DM}`), and a wire takes a member by a label of the member's name, as in KiCad. Label (`L`, KiCad's key, or the button): type the net's name in the field it opens (`SDA`, `~{RESET}`) and Enter; the label follows the pointer on the grid (R turns it, Esc drops it) until a click puts it down on a pin or along a wire, naming that net and joining it to the sheet's others of that name; `Ctrl+L` (KiCad's key), or the Global box beside the field, makes a global label, joining those of its name on every sheet; `H` (KiCad's key; on a sheet below the root) a hierarchical label, joining its net to the sheet above: the sheet's box there (each, for a sheet used twice) gets the pin of its name, on its left side for an input, else its right, below the pins there; a global or hierarchical label's shape (input, output, bidirectional, tri-state, passive; KiCad's bidirectional for a hierarchical one, input for a global one) is chosen beside the field (`crates/core/schematic-wiring.md`). Sheet (`Shift+S`, as KiCad's S, which here simulates; or the button): drag the new sheet's box on the grid, then type its name and file in the fields that open (the file follows the name: `RAM + LVDS` → `ram_lvds.kicad_sch`) and Enter: a new, empty sheet with the root's paper and title block, or, for a file the project or its folder has, that sheet shown again, its box with a pin for each of its hierarchical labels and its parts numbered anew there (`R7` where the first place has `R3`), as KiCad annotates them; a double click opens it (`crates/core/schematic-hier.md`); New sheet, in the Project tab, puts the box where the page has room and asks only its name. No connect (`Q`, KiCad's key, or the button): Q picks the tool (the key alone flags nothing; Q again puts it away), and each click flags the pin there as left unconnected on purpose, so ERC lets it be (a ring names the pin there), or takes away the flag there; off a pin's end it says so; Esc stops. Undo (Ctrl+Z) / Redo (Ctrl+Y) or the buttons; Save (Ctrl+S) writes the edited sheets, each previous version kept as `<file>-bak`; meanwhile they are autosaved as the board is (`_autosave-<sheet>`), and opening a schematic with newer autosaves asks to restore them; closing or opening another file with unsaved sheet edits asks first. Desktop only.
Update from schematic (`F8`, KiCad's key, or the button in either view; from the schematic, it opens the board first): the board takes the project's schematic as it is, edits not saved yet too, as KiCad's Update PCB from Schematic does (`crates/core/update.md`): the parts it lacks come from their Footprint fields, out of KiCad's libraries, named, valued and linked to their symbols, in rows below the board, a sheet's parts together and none overlapping (in view, to drag into place); a part whose symbol names another footprint becomes that one, in its place and on its side, its texts as you placed them and its fields kept; every part takes its symbol's value and fields (MPN, LCSC…, as the board's KiCad version keeps them: `crates/core/update-parts.md`), and its pads go on the nets of their pins, by KiCad's names, a net whose pads all go to a new name renamed with its tracks, vias and zones. The status says what changed, and which parts it couldn't add with what to do (no footprint chosen: KiCad's Assign Footprints sets one); one undo step. Desktop only.
Simulate (`S`, or the button; again or Esc stops it): the schematic, edits not saved yet too, runs in ngspice as KiCad's simulator runs it (`crates/core/spice.md`, `ngspice.md`): each part's Simulation Model, else its value (R, C, L, V, I), the sheets' SPICE directives (`.tran 1u 10m`) and the workbook's analyses, ngspice found with nothing to set up (KiCad's own). A panel below the sheet shows how far it is, then a tab per analysis: its signals by KiCad's names (`V(/out)`, `I(V1)`), the workbook's (else the labelled nets that vary) ticked and plotted against time or the sweep, AC as dB over a log frequency; drag or the wheel pans, Ctrl+wheel zooms (a right drag to a box), a double click fits, hover reads a point; an operating point lists every value. Parts without a model, or a library or model not found, are named with what to do. Desktop only.
Ask (`?`, or the button, in both views): a panel on the right to ask about the project in plain words (`What is U3?`, `Any ERC errors?`). A model on this computer answers as it comes, in Ollama (install it from ollama.com, then pull a model that can look things up: `ollama pull gemma4`), looking up the parts, nets, DRC, ERC, IR drop, git diff and simulation of the board and schematic as they are, edits not saved yet too, the files of the project's repo (README, notes, PDFs) and the datasheets its parts link to (downloaded once; PDFs read with poppler's pdftotext); each lookup shows as a chip (find ×3; hover for its calls), and the parts and nets an answer names are links that show them on the board or the sheet. Enter asks, Shift+Enter starts a line, Esc or Stop stops an answer, New chat forgets the conversation (as opening another board does), the list beside Ask picks another model (Ollama's cloud ones last: they send the conversation to ollama.com). It only looks: nothing changes. Desktop only (`agent.md`).
Flip view (`F` with no part selected, or the button, which always flips the view): mirrors X and reverses the stackup so back layers sit on top.
3D (`Alt+3`, KiCad's key, or the button): the board in 3D, fitted, from the south and above: its body by the outline, holes and stackup thickness, copper, solder mask and silkscreen in the stackup's colours, and the parts' STEP models from KiCad's libraries and the project, found with nothing to set up (`crates/core/models.md`; the status says how many, hover for why some are missing). It arrives tilting up from the plan the board is drawn in; drag turns it (let go while it turns, it turns on a moment), right or middle drag slides it, the wheel zooms, F turns it over in a sweep (Flip stays in step); a 2D tool, Alt+3 or Esc brings the 2D view back.
Measure (`M`): click start and end (on the grid or copper, as zone corners) for a live/pinned ruler with distance, dx and dy, in the units shown (Ctrl+U switches mm and mils).
Checks (`D`): first the connections still to route, as KiCad's DRC lists unconnected items (`GND: C1 pad 2 – U1 pad 2`; a click zooms to one), drawn as thin lines (the lit net's cyan) and counted in the status bar (`19 to route`; a click opens Checks); then the DRC violations (hover shows fix, click zooms and marks), by kind once they pass a dozen, each kind under its name in plain words and how many it has (`Copper too close · 72`), a long one folded until asked for, as ERC's are in the schematic; red circles on canvas. As KiCad lists them (`crates/core/drc.md`): copper too close (each net by its class), too near the edge, an unplated slot or another net's hole, solder mask openings that bridge two nets, sizes below the fab's, parts overlapping; and, as warnings in amber that no count holds against the board, track ends and vias that join nothing and silkscreen printed over pads, over other silkscreen or across the edge. The project's own severities (KiCad's Board Setup › Violation Severity) make a kind an error, a warning or leave it out.
Diff (`G`, `--diff`): compares against git (`HEAD` or previous commit) or a file; removed red, added green, click change zooms.
IR Drop (`I`, `--ir`): thermal heatmap of DC voltage drop, resistance, power loss and current density; click pad zooms, right-click sets source.
BOM (`Shift+B`; B fills zones, as in KiCad): bench checklist, one line per value and footprint; click or ↑↓ selects a line and marks its parts with the whole board in view; Space ticks it and moves on; the ticks stay for when the board opens again.
Fab files (Ctrl+P, or the button): fills the zones (an undo step when one changed), then writes `<board>-gerbers.zip` (Gerber X2 copper, mask, paste, silkscreen and outline, Excellon drills, named as KiCad names them), `<board>-bom.csv` and `<board>-positions.csv` (JLCPCB's BOM and CPL columns, with the parts' MPN and LCSC fields) next to the board, any file there kept as `<file>-bak`; JLCPCB, PCBWay and OSH Park take them as they are (`crates/core/fab.md`). The browser downloads them.
Schematic PDF (Ctrl+Shift+P, or Write it on Make's files card; agents' `pdf`): every sheet into `<root>.pdf` beside the project, in KiCad's page order, each on its paper with a frame and title block, the PDF there before kept as `-bak`; then it opens in the system's viewer. Desktop only (`crates/core/pdf.md`).
Save (Ctrl+S, enabled while the board or a sheet differs from its file, so not after undoing back to it): writes the edits into the board and sheet files, from either view, all else byte for byte; each previous version stays as `<file>-bak`.
Autosave: 5 s after the last change, to `_autosave-<file>` (the status bar then says Autosaved and when, else Edits not saved) (KiCad's name; KiCad offers it too); saving, "Don't save" or undoing back to the file removes it. Opening a board with a newer one asks to restore it. Closing or opening another board with unsaved edits asks: Save, Don't save, Cancel. A board or sheet another program saves (an agent's `save`, KiCad) shows again within a second, the view kept; with edits here not saved, the status says so instead (Ctrl+S then writes over it, keeping it as `-bak`). Desktop only.
`--mcp` / `--json`: headless MCP server and JSON CLI for AI agents.
Share (the QR button in the header, or Ctrl+K › Share; lit while on): the project on a web page served from this window, for a phone or another computer on the network to look at: a panel under the button shows the page's address as a QR code to scan and in letters (Copy, Open here), says when edits aren't saved yet (the page shows the files) or the computer has no network, and Stop sharing takes the page down; the address carries a key made anew each time, without which nothing answers; another project opened ends it. On a phone the page keeps to the canvas: the docks folded to a rail, the tabs as icons, two fingers pinch and slide the view (one turns the 3D board). Desktop only.
`--serve project [--port 8080] [--bind 127.0.0.1]`: a web page of the project for others to look at, with no window here (`--bind 0.0.0.0` for the network, its address then with a key, as Share's): its schematic (every sheet, a double click opens one), board and 3D view with the parts' models, Find, Checks, Measure, IR drop, the layers and the units, and no command that edits (no Make, Parts, Save; nothing drags; Inspect reads). The page follows the files: what is saved here shows there within two seconds, the view kept. It reads only: the project's board, `.kicad_pro` and sheets and the 3D models its board names (`web.md`).
`--bench`: no vsync, continuous redraw, fps on screen and stdout.
`--screenshot`: saves the first frame with the board, then exits.
